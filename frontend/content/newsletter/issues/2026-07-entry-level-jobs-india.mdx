Everyone keeps telling freshers to learn to code. So I pulled this week's batch to check whether the coding jobs would actually hire one. They will not.

Across every open India posting Myro Agent went through on 27 July, 7.9% said they would take an entry-level candidate. One in thirteen. Another 19.7% did not state a level at all, which in practice means someone with five years is going to eat that seat.

### TL;DR

- 7.9% of open India postings are entry-level. Senior, lead and executive together take 48.4%.
- Engineering and data is 55.1% of the market and 3.3% of it is entry-level. Business, product and operations is smaller at 39.9%, and 13.9% of it is entry-level.
- Technology has the lowest fresher share of any industry: 1.7%. BFSI runs 13.7%.
- Adobe, Salesforce, Intel and AMD posted zero entry-level roles this week. Axis Bank alone accounts for 26.7% of every fresher opening in the batch.

## The biggest band is the one that won't take you

Here is the market split by career band, and how much of each band a fresher can actually apply to.

| Career band | Share of market | Entry-level share | Senior and above |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Engineering & Data | 55.1% | 3.3% | 60.2% |
| Business, Product & Operations | 39.9% | 13.9% | 32.0% |
| Research, People & Public Impact | 4.1% | 12.8% | 47.1% |
| Design & Creative | 0.8% | 3.4% | 57.0% |

Read the first two rows together, because that is the whole issue. Engineering and data owns more than half the market and shuts the door on freshers. Business, product and operations owns less of the market and opens it four times wider.

Flip it around and it gets sharper. Of every entry-level role in the batch, 70.1% sits in business, product and operations. Only 22.8% sits in engineering and data. The band you were told to aim at gives you less than a quarter of the fresher openings.

Design and creative is its own small tragedy. It is 0.8% of the market, which means fewer than one posting in a hundred, and 3.4% of that is entry-level. If you are a junior designer in India this quarter, you already knew.

## Tech is the worst place in India to be a fresher

By industry the gap stops being a gap and becomes a wall.

| Industry | Share of market | Entry-level share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Consulting & professional services | 42.2% | 7.8% |
| BFSI | 26.3% | 13.7% |
| Technology | 12.2% | 1.7% |
| Diversified | 7.9% | 4.8% |
| Consumer & retail | 4.0% | 8.9% |
| Manufacturing & industrial | 5.0% | 2.4% |

Technology runs 1.7% entry-level. BFSI runs 13.7%. A bank is eight times more likely to look at you than a product tech company, and nobody is making reels about that.

Company by company it is worse than the sector average suggests. Adobe, Salesforce, Intel and AMD posted no entry-level roles at all. Microsoft sat at 0.8% of its own postings, Infosys at 1.2%, Google at 2.5%. Now the other side: Axis Bank ran 31.6% of its postings at entry level and Goldman Sachs ran 35.0%. Axis by itself holds 26.7% of every fresher opening in the batch, and Accenture another 17.2%.

Two employers, both of which most freshers ignore, are sitting on 43.9% of the openings meant for them.

## The role you pick matters more than the company

Same data, cut by role family. This is the column to plan around.

| Role family | Share of market | Entry-level share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sales & marketing | 15.6% | 22.4% |
| Legal & compliance | 2.3% | 11.7% |
| Operations | 2.5% | 11.0% |
| Data & analytics | 3.9% | 9.1% |
| Finance | 6.5% | 8.8% |
| Risk & compliance | 3.9% | 6.1% |
| IT & infrastructure | 12.1% | 4.1% |
| Strategy & consulting | 13.1% | 4.1% |
| Product management | 2.6% | 2.7% |
| Software engineering | 13.0% | 1.4% |

Sales and marketing is 22.4% entry-level. Software engineering is 1.4%. Sixteen times the odds, for the role family every fresher has been trained to look down on.

Data and analytics is the interesting middle at 9.1%. It sits inside the engineering and data band, so it carries the technical credibility, but it hires freshers at nearly seven times the rate of software engineering. If you want a technical first job and you want it this year, that is the door with a handle on it.

Cities follow the same pattern. Bengaluru is 35.6% of the market and 4.3% entry-level. Mumbai is 10.9% of the market and 16.8% entry-level. Bengaluru has the jobs; Mumbai has the first jobs.

## What this means for you

**Early career, 0 to 3 years.** Stop optimising for the logo and start optimising for the entry share. A bank at 31.6% will read your CV. A product company at 0.8% will not open it. Apply into BFSI, consulting, sales, operations and analytics, in Mumbai and Noida as much as Bengaluru. You are not settling. You are going where 70.1% of the fresher roles actually live, and you can move into engineering from inside a company far more easily than from outside one.

**Mid career, 3 to 10 years.** You are in the fattest part of the market. Mid-level is 23.8% of postings and business, product and operations runs 33.3% mid. If you have been sitting still because switching feels risky, this is the widest the door gets. It narrows above you: lead is 16.9% and executive 7.1%.

**Senior, 10 years and up.** Senior, lead and executive are 48.4% of everything open. The market wants you, but it wants proof rather than a title. And if you are hiring for a team right now, look at that 3.3% figure in your own band. Your pipeline problem next year is a fresher pipeline you did not build this year.

## Methodology

Figures are from Myro Agent's work analysis of 27 July 2026: 30,043 India postings across 259 employers, of which 29,473 resolved to a career band and form the base for every percentage here. Seniority is read from the posting text, and 19.7% of postings state no level, so entry-level shares are conservative by design. Career bands and industry groups are assigned per posting and per employer, and a small share of postings are ungrouped. Coverage is India-heavy and skews toward large employers with structured career pages. Myro carries no salary data.
