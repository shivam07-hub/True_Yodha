I split 14,851 openings by the industry of the company that posted them, then by city. I expected the metros to rank roughly the same way inside every industry. They don't come close. Technology puts 58.8% of its openings in Bengaluru. Banking puts 42.4% of its openings outside the six big metros altogether.

I cut the data this way because industry is the one thing most people never change. You'll change company four times and title six, and still be in the same sector at forty. That makes a national job count close to useless to you. What you want is your own row.

Each row adds to 100% across that industry's own openings. Compare along your row, not down the columns.

## Technology is the most concentrated industry here

58.8% in Bengaluru, 20.0% in Hyderabad. Four in five technology openings sit in two cities. Mumbai holds 2.9%, which works out to 49 jobs across the 60 technology employers in the batch.

The interesting part is that this isn't an absence of companies. Nineteen technology employers posted Mumbai roles in this batch, against 49 in Bengaluru, so the offices are there. They're posting 2.6 openings each in Mumbai and 20.4 each in Bengaluru. The presence is real and the hiring is not. If you write software for a living and you're in Mumbai, you're competing inside 2.9% of your own industry while telling yourself the market is slow.

## Banking is the most spread out, and this is the result people argue with

Mumbai is BFSI's largest metro at 16.5%, with Bengaluru at 15.1%. That gap is small enough that I wouldn't build a plan on it. The number worth your attention sits in the last column: 42.4% of banking openings are outside the six big metros. That's 2,107 jobs, more than the entire national technology total of 1,701.

Before writing that down I checked whether a Mumbai-heavy sub-sector was hiding inside the average, because "finance means Mumbai" is the received wisdom and received wisdom is usually load-bearing somewhere. It isn't here. Inside BFSI the data carries two labels, BFSI at 4,858 openings and Fintech at 115, and Fintech leans Bengaluru at 51.3%. Retail banking staffs branches, branches sit in every district, and the hiring follows them.

So if you're in banking in Nagpur or Indore or Coimbatore, the market is already where you are. Moving to Mumbai buys you a slightly larger slice of something that was coming to you anyway, at Mumbai rent.

## What the rest of the table does

Consulting is the largest industry in the batch at 39.0% of all openings, and it behaves like technology: 37.4% Bengaluru, then Hyderabad at 16.6% and Delhi NCR at 14.3%. Manufacturing is the only row where Pune genuinely competes, taking 15.6% of it. Consumer and retail is Mumbai's strongest row at 19.0%. Healthcare splits almost evenly between Bengaluru and Hyderabad on a small base of 174 openings, so I'd hold that row loosely.

There's a second reading worth having. Technology has 60 employers in this batch, more than any other industry, and 1,701 jobs. Consulting has 24 employers and 5,785. When you hear that a sector has lots of companies hiring, that tells you nothing about how many jobs are in it.

## What I'd do with this

**Early-career (0–3 years).** If you're in technology and not in Bengaluru or Hyderabad, moving is the single biggest thing you can change, and it gets harder every year you put it off. If you're in banking, the reverse holds and you should stop treating your city as the problem. Spend the money on the certification instead of the deposit.

**Mid-career (3–10 years).** Check your industry's concentration against your postcode before you rewrite your CV again. A manufacturing manager in Pune and a technology lead in Bengaluru are both sitting in the right place. A technology lead in Mumbai is running a much harder search than their CV suggests, and no amount of rewriting fixes a 2.9% pool.

**Senior (10+ years).** Your row matters less than who inside it knows your name. Use the table to decide which two cities your network should cover. In a 42.4%-outside industry like banking, that network is national. In technology it is one city, and you probably already know which.

## The one thing to do this week

Find your row. Compare the share your city holds against the share Bengaluru holds. If the gap runs past about ten points and you're early enough that moving is still cheap, your address is doing more damage to your search than anything on your CV.

## Methodology

14,851 job openings read from 217 companies' own career pages on 8 August 2026. Industry is a per-company attribute: every employer in this batch carries exactly one value, so no model is reading postings and guessing.

Metros are rolled up. Delhi NCR combines the Gurugram, Noida and Delhi labels, and Thane counts with Mumbai. Without that rollup NCR appears three times and reads smaller than it is.

Two limits worth stating. This is 217 large employers with their own career sites, so startups, agencies and anything hiring only through job boards are missing, and the panel leans towards consulting and BFSI. And this is one date. Concentration this sharp doesn't move week to week, but the raw counts will.
