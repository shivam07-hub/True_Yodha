# Fellowship Group Interview Prep

Date: 2026-05-24
Use case: 30-minute group interviews with 10-12 prospective interns for the Myro fellowship.

## Goal

Use the meeting to evaluate whether each candidate can become an offline growth node for Myro, not just whether they can give a polished answer.

The best fellow should be able to:

- explain Myro simply to another student,
- notice where beginners get confused,
- speak clearly in a group,
- name a real campus or community distribution path,
- ask for feedback without becoming defensive,
- turn product confusion into useful field signal.

## Recording Setup

Use Gemini for both transcription and structured notes, with consent at the start.

Do not rely only on Gemini's summary. Keep the raw transcript because exact phrases matter, especially when candidates explain confusion in their own words.

Gemini should capture:

- speaker-labeled transcript,
- one strongest quote per candidate,
- product feedback themes,
- viral/growth ideas,
- named communities, colleges, clubs, groups, or offline channels,
- follow-up commitments,
- unanswered questions.

Shivam should personally track:

- punctuality and camera/body presence,
- who speaks without being pulled,
- who listens and builds on others,
- who gives concrete examples instead of generic AI-style answers,
- who can simplify Myro without jargon,
- who has real access to student communities,
- who seems trustworthy with campus relationships.

## Ground Rules To Say Up Front

"I am recording and transcribing this meeting so I can compare feedback fairly. This is not a test of who gives the most perfect answer. I care more about specific observations, honesty, and how clearly you can explain Myro to another student."

"For the first 15 minutes, answer without using ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. I want your real thinking first. In the final round, I may allow AI-assisted framing so I can see how you use tools."

## 30-Minute Run Of Show

### 0:00-0:03 - Open

- Welcome, consent to recording/transcription.
- Assign visible speaker numbers: P1, P2, P3...
- Tell them each person will get short turns, so answers must be specific.

### 0:03-0:08 - Feedback Readback

Prompt:

"In 30 seconds, summarize the feedback you gave earlier. Do not read it word-for-word. Tell me the one thing Myro must fix first for a student like you."

What to watch:

- Can they prioritize?
- Do they understand their own feedback?
- Are they specific about mobile, upload, score, CV versions, trust, or onboarding?

### 0:08-0:15 - Use Case Reality

Prompt:

"Imagine your next internship or job application. At what exact moment would you open Myro: before making a CV, after seeing a job, before applying, after rejection, or while planning skills? Why?"

Follow-up:

"What would make you come back the next day?"

What to watch:

- Real workflow awareness.
- Whether Myro is a daily tool, an application-time tool, or a one-time CV tool for them.
- Whether they mention phone use, weak network, trust, privacy, or parent/peer influence.

### 0:15-0:21 - Virality And Offline Growth

Prompt:

"When would Myro become viral in your college or friend group? What would one student show another student on their phone?"

Follow-up:

"Name the first 20 people or one group where you would share it. What message would you send?"

What to watch:

- Specific channel: class WhatsApp, placement cell, club, hostel, seniors, alumni, Instagram, LinkedIn.
- Specific share artifact: score, before/after CV, public profile, job match, skill gap, tracker.
- Whether they understand that offline trust drives online adoption.

### 0:21-0:25 - Candidate-Led Critique

Prompt:

"If I made you responsible for 50 student signups in 7 days, what would fail first: product, messaging, trust, onboarding, or your distribution? Pick one and explain."

What to watch:

- Ownership.
- Honesty about constraints.
- Whether they blame users vaguely or identify fixable friction.

### 0:25-0:28 - AI-Assisted Round

Prompt:

"Now you may use AI if you want. Give me a 2-line campus pitch for Myro."

What to watch:

- Who uses AI as a crutch versus an editor.
- Whether the final pitch sounds human and student-native.
- Whether they preserve the CV hub story.

### 0:28-0:30 - Close

- Ask each candidate to send one concrete next action after the call: one group they can reach, one friend who should test Myro, or one product fix they would personally verify.
- Tell them follow-up selection is based on clarity, honesty, distribution access, and reliability.

## Scorecard

Rate each candidate 1-5.

| Dimension | What 5 Looks Like |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Explains Myro in simple student language without jargon. |
| Specificity | Names exact screens, moments, groups, and user problems. |
| Product Sense | Separates nice-to-have from first-use blockers. |
| Growth Instinct | Knows where students actually talk and who influences them. |
| Presence | Speaks calmly, listens, and can hold attention in a group. |
| Trustworthiness | Does not overpromise, spam, or fake certainty. |
| AI Maturity | Uses AI to sharpen real thinking, not replace it. |
| Follow-Through | Commits to a concrete next action and sends it quickly. |

Fast reject signals:

- only gives generic "make UI better" feedback,
- cannot explain when they would use Myro,
- proposes spammy outreach,
- makes unrealistic promises,
- talks over others,
- uses AI-generated language without adapting it,
- shows no real access to student communities.

Strong hire signals:

- says "students will share this when it gives them a better CV for a real job",
- identifies phone-first friction,
- asks how referrals or public profiles are tracked,
- names a group/admin/campus path they can actually reach,
- can turn one user's confusion into a field experiment.

## Moderator Notes

For 10-12 people in 30 minutes, do not let answers run long. Cut kindly:

"Good, I have the signal. I want to hear the next person now."

Use cold starts. Do not always ask the loudest candidate first. Start with quieter candidates in later rounds.

Avoid asking "Will this go viral?" as a broad question. Ask "What would one student show another student on their phone?" That reveals the actual share object.

The best fellowship candidates are not necessarily the smoothest speakers. Pick the ones who combine presence, honesty, campus access, and the ability to make Myro feel simple to a beginner.
